## Scholarly trace to an earlier recorded shrine tradition

A Waseda repository article by 星野岳義, 「坂上田村麻呂に関する伝承―菅江真澄の採集を素材にして―」, is published in 『ソシオサイエンス』20, pp.100–114, 2014-03-25, by 早稲田大学大学院社会科学研究科. The [Waseda repository item](https://waseda.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/16496) and [CiNii Research record](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050001202501216896) give this bibliographic metadata. Waseda hosts a 1.6 MB PDF; its record marks the item as a departmental bulletin paper and gives its publication data.

The PDF's indexed text exposes the table 「真澄遊覧記にみえる田村麻呂伝承」, printed p.105. Its row for 「近江国甲賀郡土山宿（田村社）」 summarizes 「田村麻呂を祀る」 and cites 「雪の出」平7, with a reference to 菅江真澄 1976, p.261, and prior research 菊池 2011, p.159. This supplies a traceable scholarly locator and a route to a late-Edo-period travel/collection record as the underlying evidence for worship at Tsuchiyama. The live PDF could not be rendered by the current reader; the table wording and printed page are therefore taken from the repository PDF's search-indexed text, not claimed as a fresh visual page inspection. The underlying 1976 edition page 261 and the cited 菊池 2011 page 159 were not read.

## Interpretation and limits

The 2014 article is later secondary scholarship that reports and organizes 菅江真澄's collection; it is not independent testimony to the tradition. The cited 1976 date is the publication/edition date of the referenced work, not the date when the belief arose. The table supports a lead that Tamuramaro worship at a Tsuchiyama shrine was recorded in the materials attributed to Sugae Masumi; it does not establish the shrine's foundation date or verify the separately attributed demon-deity episode in 『東海道名所図会』.

The entity grain remains historical person in supernatural tradition. No new name, reading, region, event date, or independent witness is established. Keep `claim_0061` open for source recheck: recover the original Koka page (or an archived copy), inspect 星野 2014 p.105, then follow its citations to the 1976 edition p.261 and 菊池 2011 p.159.
