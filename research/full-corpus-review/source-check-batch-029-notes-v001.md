| 0365 | Group 21–30, (22)「男滝の龍」 | The 六部 sees a dragon support bridge workers; the workers say they worked on rocks, then find the rocks gone. A woman identifies the waterfall's traditional lord as a dragon. `二百年も昔` is approximate tale time relative to the retelling, not an absolute year. Speaker 古泉安親; reteller 松実豊繁. |
| 0366–0367 | 21–30, (25)「葛ばば」 | The old woman instructs the 山伏 to make a kudzu stole; a great white snake appears on 笠捨山 and is repelled. Her later disappearance is told, but the text **does not say** she is the snake. The web page credits 松実豊繁 and 「奈良の伝説」; that book remains unread. |
| 0368 | 21–30, (26)「蛇の窪」 | At 上湯川の梅垣内 a snake swallows the child, then flees toward 西川の小坪瀬 after the mother throws a hoe. The resulting 蛇崩 is part of the place etiology. Recorded by 岡本勝比古, retold by 玉置辰雄. |
| 0369 | 31–40, (39)「二重滝のしろへび」 | The small white snake is called the fall's lord by the characters; after 吉兵衛 cuts it, the pool reddens. This is a narrated local lord, not evidence for a larger snake or other waterfall lord. Speaker 岸尾富定; school record, retelling 松実豊繁. |
| 0370 | 41–50, **(41) and (46)** both titled 「猫又の滝」 | Both set a two-tailed cat at 折立's waterfall, but (41) has a giant monk reprimand 三吉, while (46) has cat cries after a charcoal burner challenges it and **separate** giant-snake hunter episodes. Shared place and motif do not prove a single individual or that monk and snakes are the cat. The two entries credit partly overlapping speakers but different retellers/recorders; do not count them as fully independent attestation. The claim is supported precisely because it preserves the individual-identity uncertainty. |
| 0371 | 41–50, (43)「ダンジョウ平の大蛇」 | At 谷瀬 the hunter shoots the deer-taking snake and dies after seven days of fever in the story. His death does not independently prove a supernatural curse. Speaker 北谷辰弘; reteller 玉置辰雄. |
| 0372 | 51–60, (53)「ごうらご」 | The text itself glosses ごうらご as `かっぱ`; a woman appears at the arranged child-taking hour and leaves for water after the villagers delay her with drink. This is a narrative woman form, not proof of a separate human. |
| 0373 | 61–70, (61)「引牛」 | A giant spider threads cow and トガ tree and hauls them toward 八重佐, explaining 峠 and 滝 names. Speaker/reteller 勝山やさ子; cites 「奈良の伝説」, unread. |
| 0374 | 61–70, (62)「タンノクチガマの主」 | `ガマ` here is explicitly a **rocky pool**, not a toad. A child sees old man and woman, later glossed as white snake lord(s). The text does not resolve whether the two appearances are one or two snakes. Narrator 大谷熊吉, collector 林宏, reteller 大野寿男; 「奈良の伝説」 cited but unread. |
| 0375 | 61–70, (63)「さんばばのはなし」 | おさん disappears; only decades later does おたみ recount her alleged statement that her lower body was snake-like. The destination waterfall lord is explicitly uncertain in the quoted tale. Speaker/reteller 勝山やさ子; 「奈良の昔話」 cited but unread. The story's supernatural disclosure is not independent historical testimony. |
| 0376–0377 | 71–80, **(71)**「大池の御前若の宮」 | The thread attached to お若's nightly male visitor sinks into 大池, and the narrative calls him the lord. Villagers later enshrine **お若** as 大池の御前若の宮（お若大命神）. These are distinct figures and phases. `二百年ほど前` is approximate story time; 1889 is a later flood in the retelling, not the apparition or web publication date. Cites 「大塔十津川時報」; issue/page unread. |
| 0378 | 71–80, (73)「ソウハン渕の怪」 | A monk accepts millet cake; the cake appears in a large アメノウオ's stomach. The plot invites a monk/fish connection, but it does **not explicitly assert** their single identity. The possible drowned eponymous ソウハン is another figure, likewise not proved identical to either. Speaker 亀田文子; reteller 松実豊繁. |
| 0379 | 71–80, (76)「ガマ主と八人の杣師」 | After the woman is struck with an axe, a wounded large **ガマガエル** is found at 弁財天の渕; the scene strongly links them within the tale. Here `ガマ` in the title denotes a toad, unlike the rocky-pool sense in (62). Seven brothers' disappearance is told, not historically established. Speaker 玉置藤夫; reteller 玉置辰雄. |

**Review candidates and layer limits:** Most entries are edited retellings with named speakers, recorders and retellers, but collection dates and original printed passages are unresolved. The two 猫又 entries may share informants; no automatic merge. `ガマ` has contrasting local senses in 0374 and 0379. 0376/0377 separate water lord from deified お若. 0378's monk/fish relation is implied by narrative sequence rather than direct identity wording. No present claim needs automatic correction; the original printed collection and cited books/periodical are source-deepening candidates.
